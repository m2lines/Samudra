The original absolute-output architecture can fit these smooth fields much more accurately. Keeping a spatial-error term during L-BFGS continuation reduces high-pass error by **105–129×** relative to matched MSE-only L-BFGS, while also reducing state MSE **6.2–14.1%**. The effect repeats in two October initialization seeds and a separately fitted January example. The objective is `MSE(pred-target) + 100*MSE(H(pred-target))`, with H equal to center minus the four-neighbor mean. Both losses use the fixed normalized channels; the spatial term excludes coast/mask edges and latitude endpoints and wraps longitude. It penalizes **spatial structure in the error**, not structure in the predicted field: the exact target has zero penalty. No output filtering, residual input skip, new parameters, or temporal feedback is used. The coefficient is unchanged across contexts. Raw-grid CPUfloat64 verification reproduces all77 channel MSEs and both aggregates, with exact prepared target/mask identity.
