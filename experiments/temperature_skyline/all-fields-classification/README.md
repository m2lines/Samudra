# All-fields classification at the small model size

At matched update 15,000, EMA classification with argmax readout reduces temperature normalized MSE by **36.0%** against the 53.8M regression control (20.0% lower square-root aggregate error). It remains 10.0% above persistence in temperature MSE. Classification is worse for both velocities, and its salinity readout is limited by the fixed output range. Its all-channel MSE is 0.044525 versus regression's 0.032561. These are fixed-point validation results from one seed, not a new matched Samudra map evaluation.

All table values average normalized MSE equally across channels within each field, retaining the full-grid denominator; lower is better. Temperature is `thetao`, salinity `so`, zonal/meridional velocity `uo`/`vo`, and sea-surface height `zos`. The probability-mean readout is much worse than argmax, including temperature MSE 0.02826 versus 0.0002407. Lower categorical NLL does not establish an accurate predictive mean or a calibrated error detector.

Salinity target-to-bin-center quantization MSE is 0.019261, or 97.3% of its argmax MSE of 0.019790. At 1,850 m this error is 0.31117 versus model error 0.31237; endpoint-category occupancy is 0.384% of the wet-cell sampling measure. This target-to-center error includes both in-range discretization and finite-center saturation; the stored aggregate does not separate them, and 97.3% is a ratio rather than an additive model-error decomposition. Endpoint occupancy includes the edge categories' in-range portion. The original bins were preserved throughout this run. Temperature quantization MSE is only 4.67e-7, about 0.19% of its argmax MSE, with zero endpoint occupancy. The salinity result cannot be read as a clean test of classification capacity.

Final full-grid categorical NLL is 2.17445 nats raw and 2.11433 EMA; both best-NLL checkpoints occur at 15k. Temperature EMA full-grid NLL is 1.62699; its equal-depth mean wet-cell NLL is 2.86560. Exponentiating full-grid NLL does not give wet-cell perplexity because land remains in the denominator. Calibration and detection of Samudra errors have not been tested in this run.

The per-channel CSV includes physical wet-cell RMSE, NLL, persistence, quantization MSE, and tail occupancy. `wet_cell_rmse` corrects the area-sampled evaluation to give equal weight to wet grid cells; `area_wet_rmse` retains area weighting. For each depth, validation samples wet cells in proportion to area. Each squared error receives weight total-wet-area / (number-of-all-grid-cells × sampled-cell-area); averaging gives full-grid MSE. Dividing by wet fraction and taking the square root gives wet-cell RMSE, multiplied by the channel standard deviation. Dry cells contribute zero to the implied full-grid numerator. The renderer checks matched validation dates, channel counts, and persistence values against the regression receipts.

The completed 15,000-update run compares a categorical output head with the completed 53.8M-parameter all-fields regression model. The four-block, width-1,024, eight-head backbone and field/depth query embeddings start with exactly the regression model's seeded initial values. Replacing the scalar head with a shared 2,048-logit head brings the total to 55.9M parameters. Each example supplies a native quarter-degree 33×33 crop of all 77 state channels at two input times and predicts its center point. Inputs remain continuous normalized values, their asinh transforms, wet masks, and geographical/time embeddings. Normalization uses each channel's scalar mean and standard deviation from the reference dataset's `OM4_means.zarr` and `OM4_stds.zarr`, as in regression. Persistence holds the most recent input value unchanged for five days. Five queries predict T, salinity, u, and v at one sampled depth, with SSH active only when that depth is the uppermost layer. The target is the absolute field value five days ahead.

Each field/depth channel has 2,048 asinh-spaced centers in its normalized units. Temperature uses the 19 bounds from `torch:/scratch/jr7309/temperature-skyline-20261102/audit-local/support.json`, a complete training-only range audit; the values are copied explicitly into the linked training configuration. Other channels use fixed symmetric bounds of ±16 normalized units, chosen without validation fitting. Assignment boundaries are midpoints between adjacent centers. The first and last categories extend to negative and positive infinity; their point representatives remain the finite endpoint centers. NLL scores categorical probability mass, not a continuous density. Validation reports the fraction of targets assigned to either endpoint category (tail occupancy) and the bin-center quantization MSE so saturation or discretization limits remain visible. Targets receive no density-dependent weighting. The loss retains the regression suite's equal-channel legacy full-grid aggregation and exact corrections for depth/area and coverage sampling: land contributes zero, the spatial denominator includes the full rectangular grid, and all 77 field/depth channels have equal coefficients.

Training starts fresh and stops at 15,000 updates, with effective batch 256 on four GPUs (microbatch 32, accumulation two), totaling 3.84 million accepted crops. It uses the same data-sampling seeds, dates/crops, AdamW settings, and EMA rule as the [regression scaling suite](../all-fields-scaling/README.md). The learning-rate schedule remains the original 25k-update schedule, truncated at 15k; there is no added cooldown. This keeps the LR at each update matched to the regression control. Comparison at 15k uses the regression run's saved validation metrics at 15k, not its 25k endpoint. One seed does not establish a general advantage of classification.

Raw and EMA weights are evaluated at updates 0, 1, 50, 100, 200, 500, 750, 1,000, then every 1,000 through 15,000. Every evaluation uses the same fixed eight validation dates and 64 locations per depth/date as regression. Raw and EMA weight snapshots are retained at every validation point. Curves and CSVs include per-channel and aggregate categorical NLL, argmax-bin-center MSE/RMSE, probability-mean MSE/RMSE, persistence, quantization MSE, and tail occupancy. The probability-mean prediction is the sum of bin probabilities times their centers in normalized physical units, with no averaging in asinh space. Physical RMSE uses the channel standard deviations; the common MSE comparison uses the legacy full-grid denominator. Raw and EMA best checkpoints are selected separately by aggregate full-grid NLL; the primary final comparison uses fixed update 15k. Early regression measurements exist only at updates 0 and 1 before 1k, so regression curves will not interpolate invented early observations.
